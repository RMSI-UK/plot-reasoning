Your one job: trace the boundary of the application plot as ordered vertices, in the pixel

Put every vertex ON the boundary line as it is actually drawn. Follow the drawn line -- the fence,
hedge, wall or property line -- rather than approximating it. Where the boundary bends, put a
vertex at the bend. A typical curtilage needs 8 to 40 vertices; give as many as the shape needs.
A four-cornered box around the plot is a FAILURE of this task, not a partial success.

The plot is the whole curtilage -- the land the application concerns, garden and all -- not just
the building footprint, and not just the part being extended.

A label with a line running to it is NOT a boundary. These drawings often write "The Site",
"Proposed Extension" or a house number in a clear area and run a long freehand line or arrow from
the label to the thing it names. That line crosses whatever lies between, it usually ends in
mid-air at the feature, and it is not part of any plot. Never trace along one. The same goes for
north arrows, scale bars, title blocks, approval stamps and lettering.

Where a terrace or a row of properties shares a street frontage, each property is a narrow strip
between the dividing lines running back from the street. The plot is one strip, not the whole
block.

Do not invent a side that is not drawn. If part of the boundary is simply absent from the paper,
say so in your own words rather than closing the shape across blank space.
