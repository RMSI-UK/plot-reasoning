You are looking at one scanned page from a UK planning application file,
1988-1993: a bilevel (pure black and white) fax-quality scan, often hand-drawn, sometimes
drawn on top of a printed Ordnance Survey extract. These scans carry no colour at all --
every stroke is black, whatever colour it may have been on the original paper.

YOUR TASK, in two steps.

STEP 1 -- IS THIS A PLAN OF LAND?
A plan of land is a view from above showing plots, buildings as footprints, roads, fields
or gardens: a site plan, a location plan, a block plan, an OS extract. Anything else is
not: an elevation, a section, a floor plan of a building's interior, a construction
detail, a window schedule, an application form, a title sheet, a page of notes.
If it is not a plan of land, set is_plan false, found false, and stop. That is a correct
and expected answer -- this directory is the output of an imperfect sorter and pages that
are not plans do end up in it.

STEP 2 -- WHERE IS THE PLOT THIS APPLICATION IS ABOUT?
If it IS a plan, find the one plot the application concerns and point at it:
  point_xy  a point inside that plot, as central within it as you can put it
  box_xy    the box that just contains the whole plot
By "the plot" I mean the whole curtilage -- the building together with its garden or yard,
out to the boundaries it shares with its neighbours, the road frontage and the rear
boundary. Not the building footprint alone. Not the neighbours.

HOW TO TELL WHICH PLOT -- THERE IS NO LIST, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE.
I am not going to tell you what to look for. Whoever drew this page marked their site
somehow, and they had no house style: they may have written a label and run a leader line
to it, or written a number in it, or hatched it, or shaded it, or drawn its boundary with
a heavier or doubled or darker pen than the plots around it, or inked the building solid,
or ringed it, or bracketed it, or done something none of those words describe.
Look at the page as a whole, work out what the draughtsman did to single out one plot from
its neighbours, and follow it. Then say in what_marked_it, in your own words, what that
was. Your description is more useful to me than any category, so describe rather than
classify -- especially if it is a kind of marking you would not have expected.
Absence of any one particular kind of marking is never a reason to decline.

WHAT IS NOT A SITE MARKING
A north arrow or compass rose, a scale bar, a title block or drawing-number panel, a
council received or date stamp, the sheet border, a key or legend, and lettering itself --
including large display lettering like "SITE PLAN 1/2500". A title is not a marking.
A leader line, a road, a dimension line or a hedge that merely CROSSES a plot on its way
somewhere does not indicate that plot: follow a leader to where it stops, and the tip is
the answer.

WHEN A PLAN STILL MARKS NOTHING
Some pages are plans but single out no plot: an untouched OS extract, or a drawing where
the whole sheet IS one site and no plot within it is picked out. Then set found false with
is_plan true, and say in what_marked_it what you looked for and did not find. Do not
invent a plot.
